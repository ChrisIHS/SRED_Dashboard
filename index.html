Phase 1 of 5

Project Identification & Scoping

Define what you're solving and establish SR&ED eligibility before documentation begins.
Phase Eligibility Score 0% — Not Started
Project Name 5 pts
Use a technical name reflecting the engineering challenge — not a job name or well name. This will appear on your T661.
Technological Uncertainty 25 pts
What specific technical problem could NOT be solved using existing knowledge, standard engineering, or published industry practice? This is the #1 SR&ED eligibility question. Avoid "we weren't sure if it would work" — instead: "It was not known whether X could achieve Y under Z conditions."
🎯 Strong: "It was unknown whether nano-particle additives could maintain suspension in produced-water-based fluid systems above 85°C without phase separation — existing surfactant literature provided no data for this temperature-salinity combination."

⚠️ Weak: "We weren't sure if the new fluid blend would work in our wells."
Technological Advancement Sought 20 pts
What new knowledge or capability were you trying to create? SR&ED requires advancement beyond the current state of technology — not just improvement of your own operations. Describe what your project aimed to achieve that wasn't previously known.
🎯 Example: "The project aimed to develop a systematic understanding of the relationship between surfactant chain length and emulsification stability at SAGD reservoir temperatures — information not available in published literature or vendor technical data sheets."
Who Performed the Work 5 pts
Each category has different SR&ED cost treatment. Employees: 100% of SR&ED time eligible. Arm's-length contractors: 80% eligible. Non-arm's-length: lesser of actual cost or FMV.
📝 Additional Notes
Fiscal Year of Activity 5 pts
SR&ED must be claimed within 18 months of fiscal year-end (federal T661). Alberta IEG has a 21-month window. Confirming the year ensures you're within filing deadlines.

Phase 2 of 5

Contemporaneous Activity Tracking

Prove the work happened when you say it did. CRA requires records created at the time — not reconstructed after.
Phase Eligibility Score 0% — Not Started
How Was SR&ED Time Tracked? 20 pts
CRA requires contemporaneous records — created at the time of the work, not reconstructed. "Reconstructed later" creates serious audit exposure. We can help build a tracking system going forward.
📝 Additional Notes
⚠️ Reconstructed time records are the #1 reason SR&ED claims are reduced on audit. Even simple daily field notes with hours and project descriptions significantly strengthen your position. Going forward, add a single "SR&ED Project Code" field to existing field tickets — this one change is the highest-value documentation improvement you can make.
Field & Lab Test Documentation 15 pts
Each document type is a piece of corroborating evidence. More variety = stronger audit defense. CRA looks for a paper trail that tells the story of what you tried, what failed, and what you learned.
📝 Additional Notes
Were Failed Trials Documented? 10 pts
Failed trials are gold in SR&ED claims — they prove systematic investigation and genuine uncertainty. CRA is suspicious of projects where everything worked perfectly the first time.
🎯 Document each failure with: date, what was tried, the parameters tested, what went wrong, and what hypothesis that failure eliminated. This directly supports the "systematic investigation" eligibility pillar — one of the three things CRA must see for SR&ED approval.
Subcontractor / Third-Party Documentation 5 pts
For arm's-length contractor costs, 80% is claimable if invoices tie back to SR&ED work. Vague invoices (e.g. "consulting services") create audit risk — the SR&ED scope must be identifiable.

Phase 3 of 5

Cost Capture

Identify and substantiate every eligible dollar — labour, materials, contractors, and overhead.
Phase Eligibility Score 0% — Not Started
SR&ED Labour Costs 20 pts
SR&ED labour is your most valuable cost category. For CCPCs: 35% refundable ITC on eligible labour. Hours × salary rate must be supportable from payroll records. Alberta IEG adds another 8–20% on top.
💡 Even a partial timesheet is better than nothing. Going forward, add a simple "SR&ED Project Code" field to your existing field tickets or timesheets — this one change significantly improves claim defensibility and takes less than 5 minutes per week per employee.
Materials & Supplies Consumed 10 pts
Only materials "consumed or transformed" in SR&ED work are eligible — not general overhead supplies. Test fluids, prototype components, downhole tools destroyed in testing all qualify. Tools still usable after testing do not.
📝 Additional Notes
Overhead Method 5 pts
For most companies, the Proxy Method is simpler and often more advantageous — overhead is automatically calculated as 55% of SR&ED salaries with no separate tracking required.
📊 Proxy Method example: $200,000 SR&ED salaries × 55% = $110,000 overhead — no overhead tracking required. Total eligible: $310,000. At 35% refundable ITC = $108,500 federal refund before Alberta IEG.

The Traditional Method only wins if your actual overhead rate exceeds 55% and you have rigorous overhead tracking already in place.
Contractor / Subcontractor Costs 5 pts
80% of arm's-length contractor costs are claimable. Non-arm's-length (related parties) are claimable at the lesser of actual cost or FMV. Invoices must specifically describe the SR&ED work.
Cost Accumulation Schedule 10 pts
Monthly reconciliation of SR&ED costs is the easiest way to ensure nothing gets missed. Year-end reconstruction is common but creates gaps — especially for materials and contractor invoices not flagged at the time.

Phase 4 of 5

Project Wrap-Up & Technical Narrative

Build the technical story CRA will review. The T661 project description lives or dies here.
Phase Eligibility Score 0% — Not Started
Systematic Investigation Description 20 pts
SR&ED requires "systematic investigation by experiment or analysis." This means a structured approach — not trial and error. Document the iterative logic: hypothesis → test → result → revised hypothesis. CRA is looking for scientific method, even in a field environment.
🎯 Strong example: "We began with a base fluid formulation and systematically varied surfactant concentration across 5 levels while holding temperature and pressure constant, recording viscosity at each step. Failure of the 3% concentration trial led us to revise our hypothesis regarding CMC thresholds and test a modified formulation at 4.5%."

This shows hypothesis → test → failure → learning → revised hypothesis. That's the scientific method CRA needs to see.
Results & Knowledge Gained 15 pts
SR&ED rewards knowledge creation, not just successful outcomes. A project that definitively proved an approach doesn't work is still SR&ED-eligible. Focus on what your team now knows that wasn't known before the project.
Project Summary Sheet Status 10 pts
The Project Summary Sheet is a structured internal document that becomes the backbone of your T661 technical description. Easiest to write within weeks of the work — not 14 months later during tax prep.
Technical Staff Audit Readiness 10 pts
In a CRA technical review, the reviewer may interview your engineers or field personnel. Their ability to articulate the technological uncertainty in their own words — without reading from a script — is one of the strongest audit defenses.
🎯 Coach your technical staff to answer two questions in plain language: "What didn't you know at the start of this project?" and "Why couldn't you just look that up?" Practise this conversation before any CRA contact. The interviewer is often a technical advisor with an engineering background — plain-language, specific answers land better than technical jargon.

Phase 5 of 5

Annual Claim Preparation

Assemble the T661 package, calculate your ITC + Alberta IEG, and file before the deadline.
Phase Eligibility Score 0% — Not Started
Filing Deadline Awareness 10 pts
Federal T661: must be filed within 18 months of fiscal year-end. Alberta IEG (AT1 Schedule 29): within 21 months. Missing these deadlines permanently forfeits the claim — no extensions. Earlier filing = earlier refund.
T661 Technical Description Readiness 15 pts
The T661 requires three things per project: (1) the technological uncertainty, (2) the technological advancement sought, and (3) the systematic investigation conducted. Each must be specific, technical, and in your own words — generic descriptions are the primary trigger for CRA technical reviews.
📋 T661 critical boxes:
• Box 242 — Technological uncertainty (what you didn't know)
• Box 244 — Work performed (the systematic investigation)
• Box 246 — Technological advancement (what you learned / achieved)

Each should be 150–400 words for a well-documented project. Bullet-point style is acceptable. Avoid marketing language — write like an engineer reporting to a peer reviewer.
SR&ED Expenditure Schedule (Schedule 31) 10 pts
Schedule 31 is the financial counterpart to the T661 — it maps your SR&ED expenditures to the ITC calculation. It must be reconcilable to your T2 income tax return. Discrepancies between Schedule 31 and your GL are an audit flag.
Alberta IEG (Provincial Credit) Awareness 10 pts
Alberta's Innovation Employment Grant provides 8% base rate on eligible R&D spending, or 20% on spending above the prior 2-year average. Combined with the federal ITC, total recovery can reach 40–55+ cents per dollar of SR&ED spend.
💡 First-time filers: Your 2-year average R&D base is $0, meaning ALL eligible spending qualifies at the 20% incremental IEG rate in year one. This makes early-year claims disproportionately valuable — a $300,000 SR&ED spend could generate an additional $60,000 in Alberta IEG on top of your federal ITC.

Note: IEG counts as provincial assistance and reduces your federal ITC base — this is accounted for in the combined calculation.
Supporting Documents Package 10 pts
CRA can request this package up to 4 years post-assessment. Retain all records for at least 6 years. Cloud-based storage in an organized folder structure is strongly recommended.
